# Act One: The Threshold

## Chapter 1: The Measured Hollow

The still sat where the water was right, and the drainage fell away from it the way a garden does. Jacob packed the clay round the worm box with his hands.

"Sixty-six," he said. "I— wait—"

He came up out of the dark. Mr. Parrish had set the office up in the town below the gap, No. 4 on the square, in 1878, and the tax was 90 cents on the proof gallon. Col. Dick sat on the bench at the April term of 1879. He had $2,600 owing and three mules.

---

The corn stood three foot. There were no birds in the clearing, and he felt a cold drop in his stomach.

## Chapter 2: The Second Day

The barn was his father's raising, and the fire took the roof first. J. R. Banner stood watching it. He said, "I ain't the man for that."
