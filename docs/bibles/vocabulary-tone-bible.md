# Vocabulary & Tone Bible — *REVENUE*

**Status: v0.95 — enforceable now; one ruling pending (Q-14).**
The mechanical half of the Style Guide. `tools/check.py` reads its ban list from the `lexicon` fence in §7 below, so **edits here change the linter**. Format: `pattern | severity | note`.

Severity: `BAN` fails the build · `FLAG` warns · `NOTE` is a style reminder for humans only.

## 2. Anachronism ban list (`BAN` unless noted)

**Clinical / psychological.** trauma · psychological · psychology · adrenaline · shock (in the
medical-emotional sense: use "the blood left his face") · hypothermia ("the cold that comes before the
sleep") · compartment syndrome · arterial ("the blood choked off above the ankle") · subsonic ·
ecosystem · environment (in the ecological sense) · neurosis · shell shock · PTSD · regression ·
closure · processing (emotional) · internalized · self-esteem · identity (in the modern
psychological sense) · motivation · momentum · dynamic · synergy · network (`FLAG`: "the whole
mountain was in league" — not "the network") · infrastructure · logistics · morale · dehumanize ·
systemic · institutionalized · bureaucracy (`FLAG`: he is in the *service*, the *Department*) ·
profile · audit (`FLAG`) · data · statistics · percentage · percent (`NOTE`: "ten per cent." is
period; "the percent" as a noun is not) · optimize · efficient (`FLAG`: use "saves the work") ·
standard of living · socioeconomic · culture (as a countable noun) · heritage · tourism ·
vacationer · scenery (`FLAG` — "the view," "the prospect") · vacation · honeymoon · employment
(`FLAG` — "work," "a job," "hired") · unemployment · relief (in the welfare sense) ·
resilient · trauma-informed · civil rights · race relations · minority · demographics.

**Casual / business idiom.** okay / OK · all right (`FLAG`: "well," "yea," "so" — "all right" is
mid-century and defensible, but never "it's all right" as consolation) · allergy · reliable
(`FLAG`: "to be counted on") · current events · newsreel · phone · telegram is fine ·
mail carrier · "the mail" is fine · stationery · commute · shift work · factory (fine for the
Cranberry works; not for a grist mill — say **mill**) · employee · boss (`FLAG`: "the man that
owns it," "Deacon," "Squire") · manager · supervisor (`NOTE`: a real title in the Bureau after
1874 — fine in office mouth, never in the cove's) · foreman (fine) · company store (fine after
1882) · scrip (fine after 1882) · picket line · strikebreaker · union (`FLAG` in 1878 mountains) ·
"on the clock" · timecard · pension (`NOTE`: federal pension talk is real for *soldiers*, 1878; not
for revenue men) · insurance (`FLAG`) · real estate (`NOTE`: "land," "the place," "the improvement")
· property (`NOTE`: fine in the mouths of lawyers and in the notices) · address (as a verb to a
person: `BAN`) · contact (`BAN` as a verb) · check on (`FLAG`) · figure it out · handle it (`FLAG`) ·
situation ("the situation" is `FLAG`: our outline's favorite abstraction) · "we need to talk" ·
"things are different now" · "the world has changed" (`BAN` — the outline literally says
"the world has changed"; let the still being gone say it).

**Modern measuring / naming.** miles-per-hour phrasing; "an hour and a half" is fine, "90 minutes" is
`FLAG`; "the 1879 term" not "the '79 season"; **no Avery County** (1911) and **no Newland** (1911–12, then
*Old Fields of Toe*; R-15); **no "Whiskey Rebellion"**
for 1878–81 (it is 1791–94, western Pennsylvania) — say **"the moonshine war," "the war on the
revenue," "the trouble on the liquor tax"**; no "Appalachia" as a self-name (the region's people say
"the mountains," "this country," "the Ridge"); "Appalachian" is fine in a printed newspaper or a
Washington document only.

## 4. Syntax and rhythm rules the linter measures

1. **"Not X — it's Y" is at zero.** The outline has 7 in 3,345 words; the count is the linter's
   headline number. Keep at most **two instances in the whole manuscript**, chosen on purpose.
2. **Filter-word sweep** (Pass 3): *thought, knew, understood, realized, noticed, saw, heard, felt,
   could see, could feel, could hear, remembered, was aware*. Target: fewer than **1.0 per 1,000
   words of narration**. The outline runs at 9 "know"s and 6 "think"s in 3,345 words including
   dialogue — the manuscript's narration must be far cleaner.
3. **Abstract noun ban in the last sentence of a scene** (Pass 2): *claim, dignity, justice,
   honor, freedom, pride, respect, the old economy, collective injury, half-life, hollow victory,
   reckoning, the world, the future, the past, change*. These are the outline's vocabulary for
   meaning. The manuscript's vocabulary for meaning is: a sold copper kettle, loose mules, a nod,
   a fire in a barn the government would have had to litigate for.
4. **No Latinate inflation** (Pass 1/§3.1): cut-or-name, never upgrade. "Did not look like a woman
   about to comfort anybody."
5. **Bimodal rhythm enforced by scene type** (§3.3 + canon §8.4). Night/expectation/landscape runs
   long (25–45 words); day-work/crisis runs short (4–12). `readability.py` reports the two
   distributions per chapter and flags a chapter whose median sits in the dead middle.
6. **Four night set pieces minimum** (canon §8.4). `NOTE`.

## 5. The `not X — it's Y` tic (Style Guide §4 Pass 4, `TIC`)

Banned outright at first and last beat of any act. Budget: 2 uses in the whole book, and only where
the correction is the action. Everything else is to be rewritten so the thing is **asserted directly**.
`tools/check.py` counts the shape (`is not … — it's`, `not … — it's just/what/because/who`) and prints
the count as a Pass-4 audit.

**The fix, by example (from the outline):**

| In the outline | In the book |
|---|---|
| "He is not a villain—he genuinely believes he is restoring lawful order" | *He believed it. He also wrote it down in the ledger, in his own hand, twice a day.* |
| "This is not about whiskey—it is about who holds authority" | *The whiskey was in the barn. The question in the yard was whose word was law.* |
| "The fire is not revenge—it is terror" | *Revenge would have wanted the body. This wanted the empty night.* |
| "He is not a cold man—he is a man who has decided that structure is the only mercy" | *He sent the teams back out to finish the year. He also sat up with Matthew. Neither of these he explained.* |

## 6. Punctuation the cove does not use (Style Guide §4 Pass 4)

One em dash per paragraph maximum in dialogue (`FLAG`, budget 1). No colon before a list in narration
unless the list is the sentence's actual work — `The tools were in the loft: two barrels, a tub, a
cool trough, six feet of copper worm.`

## 8. Diction of the cove (built without eye dialect)

Style Guide §5.3: **no misspellings.** Voice comes from structure. What to actually do:

- **Parataxis.** And/and/and; few subordinator words; no semicolons in mountain speech.
- **Asyndeton at pressure.** "He has a rifle. He has a badge. He has orders." Short, parallel, no
  conjunctions — already the outline's best rhythm. Keep it for the worst moments only.
- **Concrete nouns where an abstraction would be.** Not "the harvest failed" but "the corn stood
  three foot and the fog got it in the bed of the hollow."
- **Measure-words as character.** Miller-talk for Joshua (bushel, toll, hundredweight, a ¼ off
  for dirt). Stock-talk for the boys. Water-talk for Jacob. Ledger-talk for Margaret if she gets
  it (see Q-4).
- **Negation by refusal, not by contrast.** "I won't" / "I ain't the man for that" — not
  "It's not that I won't, it's that…"
- **Biblical cadence available, dialect spelling not.** Psalter rhythm is authentic to a Scotch-Irish
  and Baptist mountain speech; eye dialect is a slur.
- **Never let a mountaineer speak an essay.** The outline's worst offenders, all to be rewritten:
  "You sold him to the government for money" · "That's not the same. Once you cross into murder,
  there's no coming back" · "Sometimes you can't stop what's coming. You just have to survive it" ·
  "It was the government's war, not ours. We were just trying to live."
  These are thesis statements wearing a hat. Put them in a newspaper, a sermon, or an attorney's
  opening — or cut them.

## 10. Tone

The register of the whole book in five lines, from the Style Guide, restated for *REVENUE*:

1. Work first. Tools, materials, and bodies are more compelling than any simile.
2. Nobody is a villain for sport. Parrish is a schedule of yields and he is *right* on the law.
3. Difficulty is welcome when it is technical and illegible when it is grammatical.
4. Stop one beat earlier than feels safe, and never explain the moral.
5. The mountain is not a symbol. It is where the water is right and the drainage works.
