## 2. Where to go in the archives, by research question

Ordered against the canon §9 list. These are the concrete retrievals — this is the part of the plan
that is *not* a web search.

**Q-12 / R1 (which county; who holds the deed).**
- Watauga County Register of Deeds, Boone: deeds, mortgages, deeds of trust, **1870–1885**; look for
  unregistered conveyances and the 1868–72 **tax-sale** entries. Mitchell County Register, Burnsville,
  for the Cranberry side of the ridge.
- **1880 U.S. Census, population + agricultural schedules**, Watauga Co. (ED lists at NC State Archives
  / FamilySearch): household ages, occupation column ("distiller"?), livestock, bushels of corn,
  value of farm products. This is where a real cove's economics come from — Jacob's neighbors, not
  Jacob.
- **1880 Census manufacturing/mortality schedules** for the same county; and the **1850/1860
  Slave Schedules** for Burke/Caldwell to know what absence means.
- *A History of Mitchell and Avery Counties*, J. A. Greene; the **Avery County Heritage** volumes;
  **Watauga County Heritage** publications; the Rowell Bosse North Carolina Room (Wilkesboro) has the
  Lewis Redmond material.

**Q-10 / R2 (court, term dates, sentences).**
- **National Archives at Atlanta (NARA)** — Records of the **U.S. District Court for the Western
  District of North Carolina**: district court minute books, civil/criminal case files, and the
  **internal revenue case files** for the 1870s–80s; also Records of the U.S. Marshal (WDNC) —
  deputy appointments, accounts, payments per prisoner. This is the single highest-value archive for
  this book, and a real finding aid lists WDNC April Term 1879 material in the Buncombe County
  miscellaneous series (C.R.013.928.3) as an example of how thin and how findable it is.
- **Records of the Bureau of Internal Revenue, RG 58** (College Park): Commissioner's annual reports
  1876–1883 with per-district tables of stills seized, gallons of spirits seized, prosecutions;
  correspondence on the "moonshine war"; supervisor correspondence for NC.
- **Papers of Green B. Raum** (Library of Congress) — the crackdown he ordered.
- **Session Laws of North Carolina, 1878–1882** (Legislative Papers, NC Archives) — the state's own
  tax and liquor law, and the **tax-sale** and readjustment acts.
- **Asheville newspaper run, 1877–1883**: the *Asheville Express*, the *North Carolina Journalist*, the
  *Asheville Citizen* (first published 1883 — the 1883 letter "so wrote an anonymous mountain resident
  to the *Asheville Citizen*" is a real source, and the reply to it is the epilogue's actual subject).
  Chronicling America has NC digits. Search: "revenue," "distiller," "moonshiner," "blockaders,"
  "U.S. Court, Asheville," "seized still," "whisky."
- Sentence practice: **judicial biography** of Robert P. Dick (FJC, NCPedia "kindly temper in
  administrating the internal revenue laws"); his papers at the **Southern Historical Collection, UNC
  Chapel Hill**.

**Q-5/Q-11 / R7 (prison).**
- Kentucky State Archives (Eddyville) and the **Virginia Penitentiary, Richmond** records; the West
  Virginia Penitentiary at **Moundsville** held federal convicts under contract in this period; the
  National Archives has Auburn and Albany federal prisoner records. Pick by research, not by guesswork.
- NC's **convict leasing** records, 1877–1890s, including the 1880s legislation that created it —
  State Papers, NC Archives.

**Q-8 / R4 (informer pay, moieties).**
- **Act of June 6, 1872**, 17 Stat. 235 and **Act of Feb. 8, 1875**, 18 Stat. 310;
  Rev. Stat. §§ 3258, 3281, 3453; **Dorsheimer v. United States**, 74 U.S. 166 (1869);
  120 U.S. 214 (1886) for the 1 Aug 1872 cut-off date.
- Instructions in **Schedules of the Internal Revenue** and the Commissioner's decisions in
  *Decisions of the Department of the Interior / of the Treasury* relating to claims for informer
  shares — the exact dollars a mountain informer could expect.

**Q-2 / R-2 (the railroad).**
- **East Tennessee & Western North Carolina Railroad Historical Society**; the **Tweetsie** records at
  ETSU (Campbell River Valley Collection, Johnson City) and the **NC Dept. of Transportation / NCDOT
  survey** files; the NPRA / **National Register** nomination for the ET&WNC (files.nc.gov, WT0010) has
  the construction chronology. **Avery Journal** county history: track to **Elk Park mid-June 1882**,
  Cranberry 3 July 1882; construction began **September 1880** (some sources say 1879) — resolve.
- Matson's/engineering reporting in *Engineering* and the **Minutes of the ASCE** for 1880–83.
- **Ario Pardee papers** (Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh / HSP) and the Cranberry Iron & Coal Company
  records for the labor force, pay rates, and the company store.

**Q-13 / R8–R10 (Reconstruction, the Klan-and-revenue entanglement, organized resistance).**
- **Trial of the Ku Klux Klan cases, Caswell and Alamance, 1871–73** (NARA Atlanta; also the published
  *Report of the Select Committee on the Ku-Klux Klan Trials in North Carolina*, 1872).
- **Kirk–Holden**: Governor Holden's papers, NC Archives; the Senate impeachment trial records.
- Matthew L. Shafer, *Blood in the Hills: A History of Violence in Appalachia* (2018) — the revenue
  violence chapter (25 agents killed 1876–1880). **Bruce E. Stewart, *Moonshiners and Prohibitionists:
  The Battle over Alcohol in Southern Appalachia* (2011)** — the closest thing to a monograph of our
  exact subject and region: ch. 4 "**They Tax Us and Give Us Negro Civil Rights**: Moonshiner Violence
  and the Politics of Federal Liquor Taxation, **1868–1876**"; ch. 5 "Civilization Requires Prohibition…
  **1870–1882**"; ch. 6 "These Big-Boned, Semi-Barbarian People… **1878–1890**." Also Stewart's
  *King of the Moonshiners: Lewis R. Redmond in Fact and Fiction* (2008).
  **Dan Pierce, *The Big Little Book of Moonshine* / *Corn from a Jar*** for "blockaders" and the
  late date of "moonshiner."
- **Peter Wallenstein** and the **WPA/Federal Writers' Project** NC guides + *Drunkard's Progress*-type
  social histories for the temperance side; the **1883 Asheville Citizen letter** as the local rebuttal.

**Q-9 (Redmond, and what the press made of him).**
- **R. A. Cobb, *The True Life of Maj. Lewis Richard Redmond…* (Raleigh, 1881)** — ECU Digital
  Collections has it online, and it is the 1881 rival pamphlet to the dime novel. Read for **how a
  mountain distiller is turned into print in real time** — this is the epilogue's mechanism.
- **"Democratic Moonshiners," *New York Times*, July 15, 1878** (the "Occasional Correspondent"
  account: jailbreak, ambush, "whipping and maiming suspected informants").
- **C. McKinley's interview, *Charleston News and Courier*, June–July 1878** (the interview that made
  him; note the two conflicting accounts of the paper's city — Charleston, S.C. vs. W. Va. — resolve
  if we cite).
- *The Entwined Lives of Miss Gabrielle Austin…* (Crittenden "Bishop," 1879) — the dime novel.
  **Use for the epilogue, and for Q-11.**

**Q-11 ("the Whistlers").**
- The thesis **"The Army and Moonshiners in the Mountainous South" (CSI Comm. pub. ADA284658, DTIC)** —
  the Army's actual role, 1865–1900, including western NC. **Read first**; it will either name an
  organization or license us to invent one in canon.

## 3. Books / articles to obtain (in priority order)

1. Bruce E. Stewart, *Moonshiners and Prohibitionists* (2011). — the spine of everything factual here.
2. *"The Army and Moonshiners in the Mountainous South"* (Leavenworth thesis, free on DTIC). — tactics.
3. Matthew L. Shafer (ed.), *Blood in the Hills* (2018). — violence; and it is the one that will tell
   us whether Act Three is too big or too small.
4. R. A. Cobb, *True Life of Maj. Lewis R. Redmond* (1881), online at ECU. — voice, print culture.
5. Dan Pierce, on "blockaders"/"moonshine" etymology and NC still counts. — vocabulary ruling Q-14.
6. Andrew L. Slap (ed.) or the standard works on **NC Reconstruction and the readjustment fight**;
   and Foner, *Reconstruction* chs. on NC/Kirk-Holden. — Q-13.
7. *The Watauga County, N.C. Directory, 1883* (reprinted online at New River Notes). — the store, the
   trades, the names, one year after our book ends. Cheap, immediate, and the fastest way to make the
   cove's neighbors real.
8. U.S. Commissioner of Internal Revenue, **Annual Reports 1876–1883** (free via Hathiport). — the exact
   per-district seizure and prosecution tables that Parrish is measured on. **This single table fixes
   the numbers in Act Three's "hollow victory."**
9. Charles H. Ambrose, *A History of Watauga County, North Carolina* — the standard county history;
   uneven, but it is where Redmond-adjacent and moonshine-era local color lives.
10. Jack Taylor / *The Tweetsie* histories and the ETSU collection for the 1882 opening.
