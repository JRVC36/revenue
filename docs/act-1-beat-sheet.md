## 2. Act One Continuity, Legal & Arithmetic Ledger

Every number, legal rule, and planted object across Chapters 1–8 must match this table without drift:

- **Parcel Map (`R-6`, `R-7`):**
  - **`Parcel A` (Joshua's mortgaged inheritance):** gristmill, overshot wheel, mill barn, cornfields, water race, and the single **cart road** crossing the forked white-oak corner (`three healed chops + knee-deep granite stone`) down onto `Parcel B`. Encumbered by **$400 principal** mortgage (paper held in Hoss Tyler's safe; **$24 annual interest at 6%** [`N.C. Laws 1876–77, ch. 91`] falling on `Sunday, 10 Nov 1878`, paid at Bakersville on `Saturday, 9 Nov` / `Monday, 11 Nov`).
  - **`Parcel B` (Margaret's 40-acre freehold):** inherited from her father; held in fee simple as a married woman's separate estate (`NC Const. 1868, Art. X §6`); root pre-1861 **Watauga** deed (sole copy after the **23 March 1873 Boone courthouse fire**, `R-15`) + postwar **1868 Mitchell County** deed kept in Margaret's cedar box. **Zero certified privy examination** (`N.C. Laws 1848–49, ch. 41`; *Scott v. Battle*, 85 N.C. 184) ever executed giving Jacob or any man an interest in her lower log barn (**forty paces** from her kitchen door).
  - **`Parcel C` (William Harper's junction farm):** tight-floored frame/log storage barn at the junction road above Tyler's store (`R-11`); site behind which **Lish Councill (15)** is shot on **4 March 1879**.
- **Corn, Whiskey, Tax & Mill Arithmetic (`Ch. 1` → `Ch. 8`):**
  - Public-mill statutory toll (`Rev. Code` 1854, c. 71, s. 6): **1/8** on bolted bread meal (`7 lb` of `56 lb` bu), **1/14** on coarse chop (`4 lb` of `56 lb` bu).
  - Market corn at Morganton (`45 mi`, two days): **25¢/bu** (`8 bu = $2.00`). Distilled yield: **~2.5 proof gal/bu** (`8 bu = 20 gal` worth **$12.50** in store trade at `62.5¢/gal`, `4 gal = $2.50`).
  - Federal spirits excise (`Act of 3 Mar 1875`): **90¢ per 100-proof gallon** (`20 gal = $18.00` cash tax — **more than the whole barter value of the whiskey**).
  - Retail Liquor Dealer special tax (`Rev. Stat. § 3244`): **$25 per year** (stamp required to be conspicuously posted in any store trading spirits, `Rev. Stat. § 3239` — Parrish's hook into Hoss Tyler's counter in Ch. 6).
- **Matthew's Store Account (`Folio 114` in Hoss Tyler's `1872–1879` Calfskin Day Book):**
  - Balance carried from Ch. 1: **$12.00** remaining on the bay mule's half-share + **$5.00** for the **December 1877** Bakersville doctor's night ride for Margaret + **$2.40** for the **April 1878** chilled cast plow point = **$19.40 total**.
  - Discharged at daybreak **Tuesday, 4 March 1879** (`Ch. 7`) in silver coin by Parrish directly to Tyler; Tyler writes across **Folio 114**: **`Settled in full, nineteen dollars forty cents, March 4, 1879 — by order of the agent. H. Tyler.`** *(In Ch. 15, *The Missing Page*, `Folio 114` is the exact skipped folio number Joshua catches in the clerk's certified exhibit schedule, `R-18`.)*
- **Federal Office & Statutory Mechanics (`VERIFIED`):**
  - **Supervisors of Internal Revenue were abolished 15 August 1876** (`19 Stat. 152`), transferring `Rev. Stat. § 3163` book-examination and summons powers directly to **Internal Revenue Agents** appointed by **Commissioner Green B. Raum** under **`Rev. Stat. § 3152`**.
  - **William Parrish** is a Washington-appointed `§ 3152` Internal Revenue Agent assigned to the **Sixth Collection District of North Carolina** (headquartered at **Statesville** under Collector Doctor J. J. Mott, 1872–82; Mott never voiced or fictionalized). Because `§ 3152` agents collect zero cash directly and hold **no statutory power to commission deputy collectors (`§ 3148`) or deputy marshals**, Parrish's courteous refusal of Matthew's appointment paper in Ch. 7 is literal federal law, not theatrical cruelty.
  - **Why a cold still convicts Jacob anyway (`Ch. 8`):** Under **Act of 20 July 1868 §§ 5, 8, 12, 44** (`15 Stat. 125`; **`Rev. Stat. §§ 3258, 3262, 3266, 3281`**), (a) possessing any *set-up* unregistered still, (b) using any still in a barn/shed/yard connected with a dwelling, and (c) carrying on the business of a distiller without bond (assessed from mash-tub lees and Tyler's ledger entries) carry **$1,000–$5,000 fine and 6 months to 2 years imprisonment** plus *in rem* forfeiture of the tract, even with cold ash in the firebox.

### Chapter 2 · *Powder on the Ridge* (Joshua POV)
*First week of October 1878 (Sat night 5 Oct – Tue dusk 8 Oct) · Target: ~3,450 words*
*Historical anchor (`VERIFIED`, E&MJ 20 Apr 1878; 10th Census Mining Report Apr 1880; Killebrew 1881): Pardee, Firmstone & Co. (`Cranberry Iron & Coal Co.`, 3,500 acres; bought 1866 ET&WNC charter Sept 1875) running narrow-gauge location sights across the Elk Creek / Doe River divide above the unincorporated Elk Park clearing ahead of the 6 Mar 1879 3-ft re-charter, using dipping needles over polaric magnetite and hand-drilled black-powder test shots on the 60–75-ft contour.*

- **Scene 1 — *Dry Thunder* (Joshua's cabin & dogtrot, night, Sat 5 Oct → Mon morning 7 Oct):**
  - **Alone Window #1 (`Guide §2.3`):** Three nights after first white frost on Roan, two flat ground-carried black-powder thumps rattle Joshua's iron shutter latch under a hard cloudless sky (**exact acoustic rhyme for Ch. 28 *The Far Side of the Ridge***, `R-5`). Bare-ankled on cold puncheons, then out on the dogtrot stones, Joshua listens across the dark millrace toward State Line Gap while Jacob's hound barks twice and goes quiet.
  - Monday morning at the mill (strict exterior): old Elihu Burleson rides down alone with two sacks of rye — his twenty-year-old second son **Tom Burleson** quit hauling cordwood on Saturday to chop sight-brush up on the Tennessee divide for northern men boarding near **Cranberry Forge**, where a wagon just unloaded Wheeling cut nails and boxed eight-by-ten window glass (`R-2`).
- **Scene 2 — *The Hundred-Foot Chain* (Watershed ridge above the unincorporated Elk Park meadow, Tue morning 8 Oct — strict exterior):**
  - Riding the bay mule up past the **Elk Park** border clearing (`one mile north of the Forge; fenced cattle grass on Elk River, no post office, no depot, no town`, `R-15`) to mark wind-thrown white oak for wheel arms, Joshua comes across a straight six-foot lane hacked through rhododendron along the state-line crest.
  - A Pennsylvania transitman in laced calfskin boots works an ash-legged **brass vernier transit** over a chiseled `X` in hornblende gneiss while **Tom Burleson** pulls a **hundred-foot steel railway chain** (`one-foot links` with brass ten-foot tags — **measuring stations and grade `C`/`F`, indifferent to county deeds or Gunter's 66-foot four-pole land chain**).
  - The transitman shows Joshua why no land compass holds true here: his **brass miner's dipping needle** pulls forty degrees down toward the black magnetic float rock under Joshua's sole. Down the west pitch, two strikers with octagon drill-steel, a tin water-cup, an iron sludge-spoon, and a **copper powder funnel** (`no spark`) are hand-drilling inch-and-a-quarter holes for the evening shot (`R-2`: *"Parrish works with a chain and a book; the railroad works with a chain and a book"*).
- **Scene 3 — *Two Ways of Reading Stakes* (Gap trail & lower mill road, Tue afternoon 8 Oct — strict exterior):**
  - Descending the gap path, Joshua crosses **Caleb** leading a pack-mare out of Beech Creek laurel. Caleb cares nothing about company wages; he points up at the raw sky-slot chopped across the saddle — a man standing by that brass tripod can now see half a mile down a draw that was blind last week (`character-bible.md`: tactical eye) — and drops two cleft-chestnut `C 4.2` stakes pulled out of the cattle trace.
  - At the mill bridge below, **Tom Burleson** stops on his walk home and turns a **Philadelphia silver dollar** over on his calloused palm in front of **Matthew** — ninety cents a day paid in cash coin every Saturday sundown for holding a painted pole plumb. Matthew looks from the silver coin down to the meal paste dried in the welt of his brogans, calculates six days' wages under his breath, and stops when he sees Joshua watching (`character-bible.md` signature action).
- **Scene 4 — *The Bakersville Slip* (Joshua's mill dam at dusk, Tue 8 Oct — Alone Window #2 → Pass-5 hook):**
  - **Alone Window #2:** Setting the race flashboards at twilight, Joshua weighs the two chains he has seen in his life — Gunter's sixty-six-foot land chain that stops at a chopped white oak, and the hundred-foot steel chain up on the divide that walks straight across boundary stones without asking whose grant lies under the moss.
  - **William Harper (`R-11`, fifty-nine going on sixty, steady, mud on his stirrups)** rides his mare up to the dam carrying a folded half-sheet brought up on the **Bakersville** mail hack: written in a neat clerk's hand, **William Parrish, United States Internal Revenue Agent**, announces he has taken the front room over the saddler's shop opposite the courthouse at Bakersville and invites the public millers and substantial freeholders of `Cranberry Township` to call on **Thursday the tenth**. Harper asks if Joshua means to ride down the North Toe with him. Joshua says he will have the mare saddled an hour before day.

### Chapter 3 · *The Office* (Joshua POV)
*Thursday, 10 October 1878 · Bakersville (`~22 road miles` down the North Toe) · Target: ~3,400 words*
*Historical anchor (`VERIFIED`): Supervisors abolished 15 Aug 1876 (`19 Stat. 152`); `Rev. Stat. § 3152` Internal Revenue Agents report directly to Commissioner Green B. Raum; autumn 1878 Statesville term opens Monday 21 Oct (11 days off), so Parrish sets the hard docket clock on the **spring sitting: Monday, 21 April 1879**.*

- **Scene 1 — *Twenty-Two Miles Down the Toe* (Road from Elk Cove past Old Fields of Toe trace / North Toe valley to Bakersville, dawn to late morning — strict exterior):**
  - Joshua and **William Harper** ride down together in frost light (`say Old Fields of Toe, never the 1911 county-seat name`, `R-15`). Physical road economy: wagons hauling chestnuts and dried apples east, Forge iron bars clinking on bolster beds, one burned-out stump lot sold for delinquent **1868 township taxes** (`R-16` bone structure in objects). Harper speaks little — four sons at home, good hay barn at the junction, zero copper on his place, wants to hear what kind of law has come up from Statesville before winter sets in.
- **Scene 2 — *The Room Over the Saddler's* (Bakersville courthouse square, noon — strict exterior):**
  - Brick Mitchell County courthouse on one side; smell of neat's-foot oil rising up the outside stair over the saddler's shop. Inside the whitewashed front room: pine table, press-copy letter book, bound *Revised Statutes of the United States*, a Spencer or Winchester repeating carbine leaned unostentatiously behind the washstand, and **William Parrish** (late thirties, dark broadcloth brushed clean of road clay, steady gray eyes, ink-clean cuffs — **zero villain sneer**, `Style Guide §1.3`).
  - Present: Mitchell County Sheriff, Register of Deeds, two township justices, an iron-forge clerk, **William Harper**, and **Joshua**.
  - Parrish speaks ten quiet, exact sentences: Washington closed its conditional amnesty this summer after men pleaded and went straight back to the laurel; the tax stands at **ninety cents the proof gallon** (`Act of 3 March 1875`); he collects no cash himself and takes no moiety (`repealed in seventy-two`); every lawful still requires survey, $300+ distiller's bond, **unencumbered fee-simple title or the written priority consent of every mortgage-holder** (`Act of 20 July 1868 §8`), and **no still may stand in a dwelling or in any shed, barn, or enclosure connected therewith** (`§12`). Because the autumn sitting at **Statesville** opens eleven days hence (`21 October`), he is filing no Cranberry informations this month; **every case made this winter goes to the third Monday of April — the twenty-first of April, seventy-nine**.
- **Scene 3 — *Mountain Civility* (Same room & saddler's stair, early afternoon — strict exterior):**
  - Parrish asks plain, courteous questions around the half-circle: who operates registered grain stills in Cranberry Township (`none`), where surplus corn goes when Morganton pays twenty-five cents (`fed to hogs and mules`), who hauls jugs down Yellow Mountain Turnpike (`nobody here has seen a stamped tierce or an unstamped jug`).
  - Every man answers politely and gives him nothing — Sheriff notes county warrants take his time, Harper says he raises hay and cattle, Joshua says he grinds public toll corn by sealed half-bushel under North Carolina law (`1/8` and `1/14`). Parrish neither argues nor flushes; he writes three neat lines in his memorandum book, noting **whose corn mill sits at the head of Elk Cove**. As the boots clump down the stair, Parrish asks Joshua which road goes up past Hoss Tyler's store (`"a good wagon grade as far as the junction"` — Parrish has already read the county map and deed index downstairs!).
- **Scene 4 — *The Ride Back Up* (North Toe road at dark, Thu night 10 Oct — Alone Window at Joshua's stable):**
  - Harper and Joshua part at Harper's junction barn in starlight; Harper remarks once that a man who brings the **Register of Deeds' index** up the stairs before asking a question is not hunting copper kettles — he is hunting **corners**.
  - **Alone Window (`Guide §2.3`):** Unsaddling in his dark mill-lot, Joshua looks down across the race where a faint red crack of firelight shows under the sill of Margaret's lower barn (`forty paces from her kitchen` — **violating `§12` every night it burns**, while Joshua's own `$400` mortgage violates `§8` if anyone ever tried to bond `Parcel A`).

### Chapter 4 · *What a No Costs* (Joshua POV)
*Saturday, 26 October 1878 · Joshua's Mill (`Parcel A`) & Cabin · Target: ~3,400 words*
*Core engine (`R-6`, `R-7`, `R-8`, `R-16`): Parrish prices Joshua's middle ground; Joshua says no; the walnut drawer holds the mortgage interest, **Caleb Audit Clue #1**, and the **1865 Knoxville Union discharge**, never linked in prose.*

- **Scene 1 — *The Agent on the Race* (Joshua's mill yard & boundary oak, Sat late morning 26 Oct — strict exterior):**
  - Late October wind stripping yellow hickory leaves into the headrace. Joshua comes out of the wheel-pit with a wrench to find **William Parrish** sitting his bay gelding alone by the forked white-oak corner stone — **looking down the single rutted cart road that drops off `Parcel A` straight to the door of Margaret's lower barn (`Parcel B`)**, where split dry chestnut stovewood is stacked neat against the logs (`R-6`/`R-7`). Parrish has not crossed the stone. He dismounts, ties at Joshua's public hitch-rail, and steps into the mill house like any customer out of the wind.
- **Scene 2 — *The Offer and the Statute* (Inside Joshua's mill house, noon — strict exterior):**
  - Wheel stopped; water hissing over the bypass gate. Parrish admires the true balance of the French burr runner stone and speaks man-to-man: he knows Joshua runs no still, keeps true weights, and owes **$400 at six percent** recorded in Deed Book D at Bakersville (`he checked the Register's index on the tenth`).
  - **The offer:** Washington allows `§ 3152` agents per-diem Subsistence and voucher payments for reliable local information — dates when mash is set, nights when jugs move down the turnpike — **more hard greenbacks in a winter than public chop-toll clears in two years**, with Joshua's name never entering a warrant.
  - **Joshua's refusal:** Two words first (*"I grind corn"*), then when pressed, a plain *"No."*
  - **Parrish's statutory turn (courteous, never loud):** He opens no gun holster; he cites the **Act of 20 July 1868** and the Supreme Court's January term (*Dobbins' Distillery*, Jan 1878): where untaxed spirits are distilled on one tract and the meal, race-water, and wagon road belong to the adjoining owner who **knowingly suffers or permits** the traffic, `conspiracy to defraud the revenue` (`Rev. Stat. § 5440` / `§ 3281`) indicts the man and `premises connected therewith` libels **both deeds**. *"A man with an encumbrance at Bakersville cannot afford a federal lien ahead of his creditor, Mr. Brennan."*
  - Joshua lifts a sack of shelled corn onto the hopper horse: *"The road's open down the creek, Mr. Parrish."*
- **Scene 3 — *The Walnut Drawer* (Joshua's cabin, Sat afternoon 26 Oct — Alone Window #1, MANDATORY CANON ANCHOR):**
  - Parrish rides down the hollow without looking back. **Joshua is completely alone in his two-room cabin (`Guide §2.3`).**
  - He pulls open the locked walnut table drawer to count the **twenty-four dollars** (`eighteen in silver halves, one five-dollar greenback, four quarters`) laid by for his **November tenth** mortgage interest (`falling Sunday this year; payable Saturday the ninth at Bakersville`).
  - Lifting the oilskin pouch exposes the two papers underneath in the same drawer — **rendered strictly as physical objects with zero explanatory glue (`R-8` + `R-16`):**
    1. **`Caleb Audit Clue #1` (`R-8` / `canon §10`):** A stitched foolscap sheet headed in Joshua's own hand **`1866`–`1869`** (years before the `$400` mortgage existed), ruled in faded brown walnut-hull ink: *To Jacob's house, Dec. — four yards stout jeans, two dollars ten; To slate and primer, forty cents; Cash for keeping, five dollars.*
    2. **`War Bone-Structure Object` (`R-16` / `canon §10`):** Beneath the foolscap sheet, a thrice-folded parchment-stiff **United States Army discharge dated at Knoxville, Tennessee, August, eighteen sixty-five** (`no company letter or regiment number printed`, `R-16`), its eagle vignette rubbed gray along the fold beside last November's Bakersville interest receipt.
  - Joshua puts the twenty-four dollars back on top, locks the brass escutcheon, pockets the key, and steps out across the footlog toward Jacob's yard (`Pass-5` hook directly into Ch. 5).

### Chapter 5 · *Keep Making It* (Joshua POV)
*Monday, 11 November 1878 · Joshua's Mill Race (afternoon) & Mill Barn (night) · Target: ~3,600 words*
*Core engine (`R-6`, `R-7`, `R-11`, `R-14`, `R-17`): Joshua states the lienholder-consent bar once; the cove chooses organized "not-knowing" with no banner name; **William Harper delivers his single verbatim "don't" sentence**.*

- **Scene 1 — *Guns and Paper* (Mill race & drying shed, Mon afternoon 11 Nov — strict exterior):**
  - Joshua rides home in cold November drizzle from **Bakersville** with his fresh **November eleventh, 1878** `$24` interest receipt in his pocket — and saw two nailed Treasury printed handbills on the courthouse bulletin board down there.
  - Jacob (45) is behind the mill fitting a new white-oak stave into a mash tub. Joshua tells him word for word what Parrish came for and what Parrish looked at: **not the kettle — the cart road and Margaret's barn**.
  - **The single statutory bond line (`R-6` — stated flatly once here, never explained again in the novel):** Jacob asks half-seriously what a government paper even costs; Joshua answers flatly that **no man can sign a distiller's bond unless his land is clear of debt, or Hoss Tyler signs a paper at Bakersville putting the United States ahead of his own mortgage** — and even clear land cannot hold a lawful still within a barn or yard connected to a house (`Act of 1868 §§ 8, 12`).
  - Jacob stands on work and right (*twenty-five-cent corn at Morganton starves a family; his father ran this branch before Washington had a clerk in Statesville*). Joshua gives the hard answer (`"Right has nothing to do with it. They have guns and paper."`). Unresolved: Jacob says the cove men are coming to Joshua's mill barn after supper; Joshua neither invites them nor bars his latch (`R-7`: *"the record of his not-knowing is a record of who he fed"*).
- **Scene 2 — *Twelve Men in the Mill Barn* (Joshua's upper mill barn, Mon night 11 Nov — strict exterior):**
  - Sleet ticking on split-oak shingles; one tin lantern turned down to a fingernail flame; smell of damp wool, mule grease, and sour corn chop. Thirteen men from the ~12 distilling households plus one neighbor with no still: **Jacob**, **Caleb (21)** cleaning the lock of his Ballard/percussion rifle with an oiled rag while men talk (`character-bible.md` signature action!), **Matthew (19)** back against the oat bin watching men's boots, **Old Man Councill**, **Rafe Winters**, **Boone Pritchard**, **Wiseman**, **Gouge**, **Dugger**, and **William Harper (60)** standing just inside the door with his eldest boys (`R-11`). Joshua stays back by the sack scale, hands folded on a half-bushel measure, saying nothing once he repeats what the bond and seizure statute says.
  - Men use `R-17`'s exact register: *making*, *running*, *blockading*, *the revenuer down at Bakersville*. **Zero group name, zero oath, zero written list (`R-14`).**

### Chapter 7 · *The Store Debt* (Joshua POV)

*Core engine (`R-4`, `R-9`, `R-10`, `R-11`, `R-18`): Ten days after Jan 18 Parrish returned Tyler's ledgers per his `§ 3163` receipt — having read Matthew's `$19.40` debt at Bakersville; Matthew approached Parrish offstage in February (`character-bible.md`: "he went to Parrish first"); now at daybreak on raid morning, Joshua witnesses **all three objects of `R-10`** in real time and cannot outrun the horses.*

- **Scene 1 — *Cold Ash and Clean Brogans* (Joshua's mill & cart road, Mon afternoon 3 Mar — strict exterior → Alone Window #1):**
  - Early March thaw; red mud ankle-deep; **seven weeks until the 21 April Statesville term**. Jacob finished his late-winter run Saturday night (`1 March`), racked every jug out of the hollow, lifted the copper cap off the still, and left the firebox in Margaret's barn stone cold while planning to split chestnut rails with Caleb up on Little Yellow on Tuesday.

  - Promised to put two sacks of fine bolted meal onto the early Cranberry Forge teamster wagon, Joshua leads his mare down the two-mile mud road in thick valley fog before dawn — passing **William Harper's silent junction barn** (`no lantern, cattle breathing inside`). Parrish counts **nineteen dollars and forty cents** in silver halves, quarters, and dimes onto the oak board. Tyler rings two halves on the wood, sweeps the coin into his till, dips his steel pen, and writes across **Folio 114** in his upright creditor's hand:
